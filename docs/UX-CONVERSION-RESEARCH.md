# Color & Scent — UX / Conversion Research Memo

_Date: 2026-08-07_  
_Scope: premium candle and home-fragrance DTC ecommerce UX; no code changes._

## Executive recommendation

Color & Scent should keep the current premium/tactile atelier direction, but shift the site architecture from “beautiful storefront” to “confidence-building scent commerce.” The best current candle sites convert by making scent legible before click, letting shoppers shop by mood/fragrance family, making bundles/gifts obvious, and putting reviews/trust close to the purchase decision. P.F. Candle Co. leads with best sellers, quick-add, scent-family navigation, build-a-bundle, reviews, and maker proof on the homepage.[13] Otherland’s PDP is the clearest conversion model: scent notes directly under title, review jump link, size choices, sticky add-to-cart, free-shipping reminder, specifications/how-to accordions, value-prop icon row, related products, and deep review filtering.[11]

## 1. Homepage architecture

Recommended order:

1. **Announcement bar:** one concrete offer only — free shipping threshold, gift cutoff, or launch incentive. Avoid rotating three promos on mobile.
2. **Sticky header:** Shop, Scents, Gifts, About, FAQ/Help, search, cart. Keep taxonomy flat for 8 SKUs.
3. **Hero:** atelier/candlelit brand moment + one CTA: “Shop the Collection.” Secondary text should answer “what is this?” in one sentence: hand-poured soy candles, mood-led scents, made/gift-ready.
4. **Shop by need:** “Best sellers,” “Gifts,” “Warm & Cozy,” “Fresh & Clean,” “Floral,” “Discovery Sets.” This matters more than a generic “Core / Premium / Signature” split.
5. **Featured product grid:** 4–8 products with scent hints, price, rating/count when real, and quick add / choose scent. Boy Smells uses scent-note snippets directly on product tiles; P.F. Candle Co. exposes add buttons on best-seller cards.[2][13]
6. **Scent finder module:** short quiz or guided tiles. Apotheke promotes “Find Your Signature Scent” with a five-question quiz, and its nav includes shop-by-scent families and a one-minute fragrance quiz.[14]
7. **Bundle/gift module:** “Build a ritual” / “Choose 3, save.” P.F. Candle Co. advertises “Build Your Own Candle Bundle” with 15% savings; Otherland leads its homepage with “Build Your Bundle,” “Buy 3, Save 10% or Buy 6, Save 15%.”[13][10]
8. **Story + proof:** keep the atelier story, but tie it to concrete receipts — wax, burn time, hand-poured-to-order, shipping/returns.
9. **Reviews / UGC:** move reviews above the footer. Homesick puts “Customer Reviews” on the homepage; P.F. Candle Co. displays reviews and customer review feed content; Otherland uses press quotes from BuzzFeed, Fortune, and People.[15][13][10]
10. **Email capture:** in-page “Join the Scent Circle” with a clear incentive, not an aggressive popup at launch.
11. **Footer:** customer service, shipping/returns, candle care, ingredients/safety, accessibility, social, newsletter.

Priority change: replace the current generic “marquee strip” and “Built Different” language with a more conversion-specific section: “Why it burns better” / “What you get in every candle” / “Gift-ready from the first box.”

## 2. PDP conversion patterns

Above the fold, every PDP should answer: **what does it smell like, what do I get, can I trust it, how do I buy?** Use Otherland’s PDP as the closest benchmark: title, price, notes (“Tennis Balls • Cut Grass • Cucumber”), review count jump link, size selector, sticky add-to-cart, shipping threshold, accordions, value icons, related products, and review filters are all present on one page.[11]

Recommended Color & Scent PDP anatomy:

- Gallery: product, lit/lifestyle, label close-up, scale-in-hand, packaging/gift shot.
- Buy panel order: collection/badge → product name → price → 1-line scent summary → rating/review count → scent/size/lid options → quantity + Add to Cart → free-shipping progress → delivery/returns/security row.
- Scent notes: keep top/heart/base, but do not bury them below the fold only; include a 3-note shorthand beside the CTA.
- Accordions: Description, Scent Notes, Ingredients/Wax, Burn & Care, Shipping/Returns, Gifting.
- “Complete the ritual”: wax melts, wick trimmer, 3-candle bundle, or seasonal companion scent.
- Reviews: include rating distribution, verified-buyer status, search/filter, “with media,” and review topics. Otherland’s review module supports search, rating filter, media filter, popular topics, sort, detailed ratings for burn time/fragrance strength, and verified buyer labels.[11]

Avoid: forcing shoppers to choose between too many similar scent/lid variants without explanatory copy. Variant selection should reveal “smells like / best for / strength / room fit.”

## 3. Scent selection UX

Scent is the hard ecommerce problem. Treat it as navigation, not only product copy.

Recommended patterns:

- **Shop by scent family:** Warm/Spiced, Woody, Floral, Fresh/Clean, Gourmand, Citrus/Fruity. Voluspa’s footer/category taxonomy exposes scent groups including Aquatic, Citrus/Fruity, Floral, Gourmand, Green, Warm/Spiced, and Woody.[12]
- **Shop by use case:** Bedroom wind-down, dinner party, clean kitchen, workday reset, gift-safe crowd pleasers.
- **Product card scent line:** three notes, not prose. Example: “Amber • Tonka • Vanilla.” Boy Smells and Otherland both make note trios visible early.[2][11]
- **Scent quiz:** launch with a lightweight guided module before building a complex quiz: “Choose a mood,” “Choose a room,” “Choose sweet/fresh/woody/floral,” “Choose intensity.” Apotheke’s one-minute quiz is a useful current precedent.[14]
- **Discovery set:** 3 minis or wax melts as a low-risk first purchase. If Printify constraints limit minis, make a “Scent Explorer Wax Melt Set.”
- **Scent comparison table:** strength, sweetness, freshness, warmth, best room, best season.

## 4. Gift flow

Gift flow should be a top-level purchase path, not just a collection tag.

Recommendations:

- Add top nav item **Gifts** with landing sections: Best gifts under $30, under $50, Gift sets, Housewarming, Thank you, Birthday, Wedding/host, Self-care.
- PDP gift widgets: “Gift-ready packaging,” “Add gift note,” “Hide prices on packing slip” if operationally true, and shipping cutoff language.
- Bundle builder: “Pick 3 scents” with savings and a gift framing. Otherland and P.F. Candle Co. both use bundle-building as a primary homepage conversion lever.[10][13]
- Gift cards only if fulfillment/customer support is ready.
- Occasion copy matters: Homesick’s homepage uses occasion-led merchandising like “For Every ‘I Do’” and back-to-school framing.[15]

## 5. Subscription / bundling

Do **bundles before subscriptions**. Subscriptions can feel premature for a new POD candle brand unless fulfillment, scent rotation, and churn support are solved.

Priority:

1. Buy 2 / Buy 3 thresholds to lift AOV.
2. Discovery or “Scent wardrobe” bundle.
3. Gift set builder.
4. Subscription later: “Seasonal Scent Club” quarterly, not monthly, with skip/cancel clarity.

Precedents: Otherland’s bundle offer is explicit — buy 3 save 10%, buy 6 save 15%; P.F. Candle Co. promotes a build-your-own candle bundle with 15% savings.[10][13]

## 6. Email capture

Use layered capture, but keep it premium:

- Footer/in-page: “Join the Scent Circle — 10% off your first order + seasonal scent notes.” Apotheke uses a 10% newsletter incentive; Otherland uses “Subscribe and #EscapeToOtherland.”[14][10]
- Quiz-gated capture: ask for email after showing results, not before.
- Gift-guide capture: “Send me the candle gift guide.”
- Post-purchase: candle care + review request + refill/bundle recommendation.
- Avoid launch popup unless it is delayed and easy to dismiss; Design System’s newer “no popup at launch” stance is stronger than older exit-intent guidance.

## 7. Reviews / UGC

Launch honestly: do not fake review volume. If there are no real reviews, use founder notes, beta tester quotes clearly labeled, and UGC prompts.

Recommended progression:

- Product cards: show ratings only when real count exists.
- PDP: collect star rating, written review, scent strength, burn time, room used, gift/self, photo/video.
- Homepage: show 3–6 best review excerpts once real.
- UGC: “Show us your evening ritual” grid after real customers exist.
- Moderation: Otherland’s live reviews demonstrate the need for spam moderation; one visible review contained unrelated spam-like content, so do not blindly render every review without moderation.[11]

## 8. Trust signals

Use quiet, concrete trust signals close to CTAs:

- Free shipping threshold.
- Secure checkout/payment icons.
- Shipping timing: “Poured to order; ships in X–Y business days” if true.
- Returns/replacements: be honest about burned candles and damaged shipments.
- Wax/wick/material facts.
- Burn time.
- Made/fulfilled location if true.
- Accessibility link and visible support contact.
- Gift readiness if operationally true.

Byredo’s footer groups luxury ecommerce trust services — free shipping, 30-day returns, free samples, gift wrapping, digital gift card, personalized message, secure payments — which is a useful checklist even if Color & Scent implements a simpler version.[16]

## 9. Mobile UX

Mobile should be the primary design target.

Recommendations:

- Header: 56px, hamburger/search/cart, persistent cart count.
- First viewport: product image, product name, price, scent shorthand, reviews, CTA visible without excessive scroll.
- Sticky bottom Add to Cart after inline CTA scrolls away.
- Horizontal scent chips must have visible scroll hint and not trap focus.
- Carousels need dots/counter and swipe; hide non-visible slides from tab order.
- Cart drawer: subtotal, free-shipping progress, editable quantity, checkout button, continue shopping.
- Avoid heavy autoplay video or large motion on mobile; use still editorial imagery.

Otherland’s PDP uses a product image carousel, thumbnail carousel, quantity controls, sticky add-to-cart region, and review jump behavior, which is the right mobile conversion model to adapt in a calmer visual style.[11]

## 10. Accessibility

The existing Accessibility Guide is one of the strongest current docs and should stay normative. Add these ecommerce-specific requirements:

- Scent/lid choices should be radio groups with accessible names and selected states.
- Star ratings need one text alternative, not five announced stars.
- Free-shipping progress needs a real accessible name/value.
- Review filters/search/sort must be keyboard usable.
- Cart drawer must trap focus and return focus to trigger.
- Do not rely on accessiBe-style overlays as a substitute for native accessibility. Apotheke and Snif display accessiBe controls, but Color & Scent’s own guide correctly favors semantic implementation over overlay remediation.[14]

## 11. Cart / checkout recommendations

Cart drawer:

- Open drawer after add-to-cart on desktop; on mobile show drawer or sticky toast with “View cart.”
- Show selected scent/lid/size in each line item.
- Quantity editing, remove with undo, subtotal, shipping/tax calculated at checkout.
- Free-shipping progress and “add one more” cross-sell.
- Gift note option if supported.
- Payment icons and secure checkout line.
- Do not surprise with shipping/production timing at checkout; state it on PDP and cart.

Checkout:

- Express pay buttons high on checkout if platform supports them.
- Guest checkout default.
- Address autocomplete and correct mobile input modes.
- Order summary remains visible/collapsible on mobile.
- Post-purchase page: candle care, tracking expectations, review/UGC prompt.

## Prioritized change list

### P0 — before launch

1. Rework homepage architecture around Shop by Scent, Gifts, Best Sellers, Bundle, Reviews/Proof, and Email.
2. Add scent shorthand to every product card and above-fold PDP buy panel.
3. Implement honest trust rows: shipping threshold, production/shipping timing, returns/replacements, secure checkout.
4. Build a gift landing path and gift-ready PDP language.
5. Make cart drawer conversion-complete: free shipping progress, line-item variant clarity, subtotal, checkout, cross-sell.
6. Remove any fake review counts or placeholder “400+ reviews.”

### P1 — early post-launch

1. Add scent quiz / scent finder.
2. Add discovery set / wax melt sampler.
3. Add review collection fields: scent strength, burn time, room, gift/self, media.
4. Add UGC/gallery after real customer content exists.
5. Add bundle builder beyond static bundles.

### P2 — after operational proof

1. Subscription / seasonal scent club.
2. Personalization/custom builder if margins and fulfillment complexity are proven.
3. Press logos only when real.

## Existing Color & Scent docs that may be stale, generic, or conflicting

- **DESIGN-BRIEF.md:** partially stale. It recommends Deep Indigo + Blush Rose and claims “all hex codes, fonts, and pricing verified” from Aug 3; DESIGN-SYSTEM.md now supersedes this with charcoal/cream/amber. Also several takeaways are broad visual analysis, not conversion UX.
- **HOMEPAGE-WIREFRAME.md:** directionally good visually, but conversion-generic. “Marquee strip,” “Built Different,” and “The People Have Spoken 400+ reviews” are risky without real proof. It underweights scent finder, gift path, bundle economics, and source-specific trust.
- **PDP-WIREFRAME.md:** strong base, but needs more conversion detail for review filtering, gift option, scent strength/room fit, cross-sells, and operational truth around POD shipping/returns. It assumes review counts and 30-day returns that must be verified.
- **COMPONENT-LIBRARY.md:** generally useful, but some terms are generic (“Built Different,” badges like “Hand Poured”) unless tied to verifiable claims. Ensure quick-add and product card patterns include scent choice clarity.
- **RESPONSIVE-SPEC.md:** strong and current; keep.
- **ACCESSIBILITY-GUIDE.md:** strong and current; keep as normative. Add ecommerce review/filter/cart specifics from this memo.
- **RELAUNCH-PLAN.md:** operationally useful, but UX scope is too narrow under “Rebrand UI + copy.” It should explicitly include gift flow, bundle strategy, cart/drawer, and review/trust readiness.
